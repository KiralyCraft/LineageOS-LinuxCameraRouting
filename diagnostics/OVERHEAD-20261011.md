# FFmpeg stream-copy overhead after reboot, 2026-10-11

Installed APK and Magisk module: 0.1.3, protocol v2. The persistent policy check
passed, all four unique endpoints registered, and SELinux remained enforcing.
Phone awake, charging, all eight cores online. Helper CPU eligibility 0-7 in
Android's foreground cpuset; camera provider and cameraserver in camera-daemon.
No CPU, GPU, scheduler or power-policy settings were changed.

Each case used 3 seconds of startup/warmup followed by an 18-second steady
measurement. FFmpeg ran as Linux UID 4000 and streamed raw V4L2 packets with
`-c:v copy -f null /dev/null`; no decoding, encoding, filters or video files.
Progress/log files were stored in RAM-backed /tmp. Process CPU deltas come from
/proc/PID/stat and monotonic elapsed time; 100% means one fully occupied CPU
core, not the whole eight-core SoC. CPU percentages do not normalize for core
frequency or performance. Idle baseline for all four Android processes was 0%
over eight seconds. These are short tests, not thermal-soak or power measurements.

Timestamp fps comes from delivered packet-count deltas divided by V4L2 timestamp
deltas. Independent wall-clock estimates were within 0.17 fps of the 30-fps
request and 0.13 fps of the 60-fps request; FFmpeg progress quantization is
100 ms. Final FFmpeg summaries also showed sustained real-time delivery.
SIGINT intentionally ended each case; exit 255 accompanies FFmpeg's explicit
"Exiting normally, received signal 2", not a capture fault. No capture error
occurred. The root broker used 0.00% CPU in every steady window.

The bridge's existing counters measure plane packing plus DMA-BUF cache-sync
wall time, excluding source acquisition and initial mapping. Counters are
cumulative across streams. Differencing frame-weighted means (rounded in logs)
gives approximately 3.92 ms/frame at 720p30, 6.19 at 1080p30, 4.92 at 1080p60,
and 13.90 at 4K30 for the main camera. These are whole-session estimates and
include startup frames. The largest recorded copy-stage time was 39.83 ms in
the 4K case; average frame rate is not proof of perfectly uniform delivery.
One no-destination-buffer drop was counted over 4,923 completed producer frames;
this counter does not include frames discarded internally by acquireLatestImage
or the HAL and must not be interpreted as total sensor frame loss.

FFmpeg overhead is low. The helper's raw transfer is measurable, especially at
4K, but it sustained the requested modes in these runs. Sony's camera provider
accounts for more CPU than the helper, but no Android-only control was measured,
so its load cannot be claimed to be irreducible capture cost. The null sink also
does not predict the extra cost of application image processing or encoding.

After the tests the main node was restored to 1920x1080@30; other tested nodes
were also at 1080p30. Android showed no active camera clients and the helper
returned to Ready. No camera images were captured or saved for this benchmark.

Reproduce a 20-second default-mode stream-copy run:

```sh
ffmpeg -hide_banner -f v4l2 -input_format yuv420p -video_size 1920x1080 \
  -framerate 30 -i /dev/linux-camera/rear-2 -t 20 -c:v copy -f null /dev/null
```
